# Stream all day, think on the charger

As of October 2026, the best way to run Bee audio entirely on an iPhone 17 Pro is a **capture-then-batch pipeline with a thin live layer**. While FreeBee is in the background it does four things: receive the Bee's roughly 4 KB/s of AES-128-CTR-encrypted AAC, decrypt it with CommonCrypto, check the ADTS frames and append them unchanged to disk, and at most run a CPU-only Silero VAD. The heavy stages run later: transcription with NVIDIA Parakeet TDT v3 through FluidAudio (Apple's SpeechTranscriber is the fallback and the candidate for live drafts), pyannote community-1 diarization matched against an enrolled "Me" voiceprint, Foundation Models extraction, and search indexing. They run in the foreground, in a user-started continued task, or overnight on the charger. iOS 27 forces this split. **Background Neural Engine access now requires a new entitlement** that FreeBee doesn't have. Neural Engine memory now counts against the app. There is no background GPU on iPhone. Background Foundation Models requests "might be throttled or canceled". Only an on-power `BGProcessingTask` turns off the CPU monitor. Bandwidth isn't a constraint, since 32 kbps is a sliver of BLE capacity. **Staying connected is the hard part**: the Bee keeps no audio on the device, so every disconnect loses that audio for good. For understanding, the iPhone 17 Pro runs Apple's AFM 3 Core Advanced model, but test runs show a **4,096-token context**. Summaries therefore have to be map-reduced, and a larger open model (Qwen3.5-4B on MLX) should be kept for daily rollups in the foreground. Reviewers of Bee, Omi, Limitless and Friend point to the same product rules: produce fewer outputs, link each one to its source, suppress TV and podcast audio, and keep mute one action away. One caveat: **none of this has been measured on the user's phone, and FreeBee's iOS audio path (decrypt plus ADTS) doesn't exist yet**. The first job is to build ingest and run a 16-hour locked-phone soak test and a one-day engine probe.

## The Bee streams 4 KB/s and keeps none of it

Our Bee Pioneer on firmware 0.6.1 sends **MPEG-4 AAC-LC in ADTS at 16 kHz, mono, no CRC, about 32 kbps**. Each frame is 1,024 samples (64 ms), carried in fixed 495-byte notifications (4-byte sequence number plus 491 bytes of ciphertext) at the 498-byte MTU macOS negotiated. A 45 s capture produced 366 notifications and 698 frames with zero losses, a median inter-arrival time of 120 ms and a worst gap of 210 ms (our capture (`captures/20261020-201740-raw.jsonl`); [our PROTOCOL.md](../../../docs/PROTOCOL.md)). Frames are variable-length (213–345 bytes), so about 1.9 frames straddle each packet. One lost notification corrupts roughly 130–190 ms of audio, and the reassembler has to resync on the next syncword. Ours does, and so does Omi's desktop Bee connector, which treats a frame length under 7 as a false sync and advances one byte ([Omi BeeDeviceConnection.swift](https://github.com/BasedHardware/omi/blob/main/desktop/macos/Desktop/Sources/Bluetooth/Connections/BeeDeviceConnection.swift)). Bitrate differs between units. jkmills' unit on 0.6.5 reports 8,000 bps, and a Frida hook in the official app measured 468 frames in 29.9 s, about 8 kbps ([jkmills PROTOCOL.md](https://github.com/jkmills/bee-wearable-ble/blob/main/PROTOCOL.md); [Omi #20828](https://github.com/BasedHardware/omi/issues/20828)). The parser therefore can't assume a packet size or a frame rate per packet. At 32 kbps the stream is near-transparent wideband speech. Opus at the same bitrate costs only 0.1–0.2% absolute WER in GigaSpeech ([GigaSpeech paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.06909)), while 8 kbps is noticeably lossy. Leave our unit at 32000 and treat `SetBitrate` as an experiment; it has never been tested live ([jkmills README](https://github.com/jkmills/bee-wearable-ble/blob/main/README.md)).

The most consequential device fact is a negative one. **The Bee has no audio archive and no download protocol.** The full command set (C001–C01D) has no list or fetch command, USB only charges, and Amazon says "no audio is ever stored" ([jkmills PROTOCOL.md](https://github.com/jkmills/bee-wearable-ble/blob/main/PROTOCOL.md); [Bee blog](https://bee.computer/blog/private-ai)). The official app never transcribed locally. Static analysis shows it pushed each ADTS frame live over a Socket.IO WebSocket to server-side STT, and its bundled Silero model only drove a voice-energy meter ([jkmills PROTOCOL.md](https://github.com/jkmills/bee-wearable-ble/blob/main/PROTOCOL.md)). A 2025 reviewer's recollection that Bee "transcribed on the phone" doesn't hold up against that code. FreeBee isn't reimplementing a local design. It replaces a cloud design, and anything said while the phone isn't connected is lost. In state 1 (RECORDING) the firmware streams silence too: quiet stretches around −37 to −43 dBFS arrived alongside speech in our capture. Gating has to happen on the phone.

Three protocol behaviours that the earlier notes disagreed on can now be settled. First, **the physical button is a firmware-level recording toggle**. jkmills confirmed on hardware that a single press turns the LED and the stream on and off, and that with a session open the device pushes `A003` on each toggle. The official app's button plugin sends no BLE command back. It only relays press, release and double-press events to the backend ([jkmills PROTOCOL.md](https://github.com/jkmills/bee-wearable-ble/blob/main/PROTOCOL.md)). So `A003` is the source of truth for recording state, a single press belongs to the device, and only double press (`A001`) and hold (`A002`) are free for FreeBee to map. Second, the reconnect sequences differ. Across seven reconnects, the official app sent `GetDeviceID → GetSecureIds → GetDeviceState → GetFirmwareVersion → GetBattery → GetBitrate → GetTxLevel → SetDeviceState(MUTE) → GetSessionNonce` and then enabled the audio CCCD. It sent `SetEncryptionKey` only at pairing ([jkmills PROTOCOL.md](https://github.com/jkmills/bee-wearable-ble/blob/main/PROTOCOL.md)). FreeBee instead runs `SetEncryptionKey(real token) → GetSessionNonce` on each connection, before the first mute or unmute ([BeeStore.swift](../../../app/FreeBee/FreeBee/Model/BeeStore.swift); [BeeClient.swift](../../../app/FreeBee/BeeKit/Sources/BeeKit/BeeClient.swift)). That path is hardware-verified, and our protocol table treats `0x8004` as "key accepted (re-key)", not as an error ([our PROTOCOL.md](../../../docs/PROTOCOL.md)). The two sequences don't actually conflict. The firmware probably keeps the key from pairing, and re-sending the real token is accepted. FreeBee should keep its verified path, test the minimal official path as an experiment, and declare a session "ready" only when the first decrypted bytes start with the ADTS header `0xFFF1`. That known-plaintext check proves the key and nonce are right before any audio is decoded. Because the official app mutes on every reconnect, FreeBee must explicitly re-arm `SetDeviceState(1)` after reconnecting when the user wants capture, then confirm it with `GetDeviceState` or `A003`. Third, sleep states 2 (hardware VAD) and 4 (software VAD) are real, can be set without a session, and keep the link up. But **nobody has observed them wake on speech and start streaming**. The Omi pendant is a warning: in one 10.5-hour trace its microphone went into hardware VAD sleep and "never woke" while the link stayed up ([Omi #11307](https://github.com/BasedHardware/omi/issues/11307)). Until we test them, states 2 and 4 are experiments, not the default, and phone-side VAD is the authority.

How long the Bee's battery lasts while streaming continuously is **unknown**. Amazon claims "up to 7 days (up to 160 hours)" for the Bee Frontier, alongside a default microphone auto-off after 5 minutes to 24 hours of silence ([Bee Frontier](https://bee.computer/products/frontier)). A competing vendor claims 1.5–2 days of active listening, which carries a conflict of interest ([UMEVO](https://www.umevo.ai/blogs/ume-all-posts/bee-ai-pendant-complete-review-features-battery-life-and-pricing-2026)). One App Store reviewer reports "three long days of work" ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/bee-your-personal-ai/id6480349491)). None of these is a measurement of state 1 at 32 kbps. Firmware is the other device-side threat. Firmware 0.6.1 introduced audio encryption, which broke Omi's direct Bee support ([Omi PR #5744](https://github.com/BasedHardware/omi/pull/5744)). An over-the-air update or re-pairing through the official app could invalidate our captured token, so the official app should stay deleted. Our unit's second MCUboot slot still holds 0.5.0, which Omi's account suggests is pre-encryption, but booting it would need SMP write operations that could brick the device. That is a last resort only.

## Reconnects, not bandwidth, decide how much of the day survives

At about 4 KB/s, the Bee needs a small fraction of what even pessimistic iPhone links deliver. One weak iPad report still managed about 8 KB/s ([Apple Developer Forums 773555](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/773555)). The app can't tune the link anyway. Apple DTS says connection parameters are negotiated by the accessory ("There is nothing you can do in an app that will change those"), and the iPhone, as central, picks the MTU ([forums 773555](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/773555); [forums 665058](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/665058)). The iPhone 17 Pro's negotiated MTU, interval and PHY with the Bee are unmeasured. Logging `maximumWriteValueLength(for: .withoutResponse)` and every notification's size, plus one PacketLogger trace, will answer it. Lost audio should be computed from **wall-clock time against decoded frames** (each frame is exactly 64 ms), not from a jump in sequence number times a fixed constant. That method stays correct at any MTU or bitrate.

Link continuity is what fails in practice. Omi has the only detailed public field data for an always-on BLE audio pendant on iOS (iPhone 15 Pro Max, iOS 26.6). **19 of its last 20 disconnects were background `connection_timeout` at RSSI −91 to −97**, it logged 983 lifetime reconnects, and a separate control day showed 11 disconnects in about 10.5 hours ([Omi #11307](https://github.com/BasedHardware/omi/issues/11307)). The same report shows that iOS will keep a connected BLE app running for **16 h 19 min in the background** without CPU-monitor kills. Omi's 51% battery attribution came from stacked keep-alives (an `audio` session, a foreground task, a 5 s battery poll, 200 ms reconnects with no backoff) and from a jetsam kill at about 637 MB resident, not from the stream itself ([Omi #11307](https://github.com/BasedHardware/omi/issues/11307)). Omi's maintainers concede that "no phone energy has ever been measured" for their background fixes ([Omi #19565](https://github.com/BasedHardware/omi/issues/19565)). Omi proposed a rule FreeBee should adopt: **a connection is not readiness until fresh frames arrive.** As a rough estimate, about 11 in-range drops a day with 5–10 s of recovery each would lose only 1–2 minutes of a 16-hour day. Time spent out of range is lost minute for minute, and reconnect storms dominate the bad cases.

Apple's reconnect tools favour a **pending connection over scanning**. Connection requests "do not time out", so FreeBee should call `connect()` again the moment a link drops ([Core Bluetooth guide, archived](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/NetworkingInternetWeb/Conceptual/CoreBluetooth_concepts/CoreBluetoothBackgroundProcessingForIOSApps/PerformingTasksWhileYourAppIsInTheBackground.html)). DTS warns that background scan results are coalesced, and says the fix is to "connect with your accessory and directly interact with it" ([forums 840468](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/840468)). The earlier notes disagreed about force-quit. **Since iOS 26, AccessorySetupKit apps like FreeBee are relaunched after a force-quit and after a Control Center Bluetooth toggle** (TN3115 note 5), and two DTS engineers reaffirmed this in August and September 2026 ([TN3115](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technotes/tn3115-bluetooth-state-restoration-app-relaunch-rules); [forums 844477](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/844477); [forums 840468](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/840468)). The older statement that iOS never relaunches a force-quit app still holds for non-ASK apps and before the first unlock after a reboot. One unresolved iOS 26.5 report says the relaunch didn't fire, but that app relied on advertisement scanning rather than a pending connect ([forums 840468](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/840468)). FreeBee already has the right foundations: `bluetooth-central` as its only background mode, a constant restore identifier, a Keychain token readable after first unlock, and an RSSI poll gated to the foreground ([KeyStore.swift](../../../app/FreeBee/FreeBee/Model/KeyStore.swift); [BeeStore.swift](../../../app/FreeBee/FreeBee/Model/BeeStore.swift)). FreeBee should **not** add an `audio` keep-alive. Notifications already keep the app scheduled, and Apple says each background mode should be used only for "its intended purpose" ([forums 685525](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/685525)).

iOS 27 makes the needed instrumentation cheap. The new Swift `MetricManager` delivers `MetricReport` and `DiagnosticReport` objects, including a new `MemoryExceptionDiagnostic` ([iOS 27 release notes](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/ios-ipados-release-notes/ios-ipados-27-release-notes)). The SDK's `BackgroundTerminationMetric` counts memory, high-CPU, system-pressure and watchdog terminations separately. Together with FreeBee's own log of packets and link events, these will show within a day whether the "streams all day while locked" premise holds for a `bluetooth-central`-only app on iOS 27.0.1. No one has published that result.

## iOS 27 lets FreeBee listen all day but not think all day

The earlier notes contradicted each other on where heavy ML can run. One kept it out of the BLE wake path entirely, one ran Parakeet "in small batches while the app is alive in the background", and one ran Foundation Models extraction "near real time" in the same background mode. The iOS 27 release notes settle it: "**The system now restricts background access to the Neural Engine, similar to GPU usage restrictions** … Neural Engine memory usage is now attributed to your app process … Access to the Neural engine when your app is in the background requires the new entitlement: 'com.apple.developer.background-tasks.continued-processing.inference'" ([iOS 27 release notes](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/ios-ipados-release-notes/ios-ipados-27-release-notes)). The entitlement page adds that it is required "for any Neural Engine access while your app is in the background, regardless of whether it's running a continued background task", via Core AI, Core ML or MPSGraph ([Background Inference entitlement](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/entitlements/com.apple.developer.background-tasks.continued-processing.inference)). FreeBee's entitlements file contains only an app group ([Bee.entitlements](../../../app/FreeBee/Support/Bee.entitlements)). FluidAudio's claim that Parakeet "runs on the ANE (so iOS background transcription works)" is therefore **false for FreeBee as configured** ([ParakeetUltra.md](https://github.com/FluidInference/FluidAudio/blob/main/Documentation/ASR/ParakeetUltra.md)). Nobody has published whether an app without the entitlement gets an error or a silent CPU fallback. The one production project that reacted, joeblau/voice, steps a stage from ANE to CPU to SpeechTranscriber after "two errors in a row, or a p95 above 80% of a stage's budget". Its author calls the mitigation "provisional", since it was tested only on simulators ([joeblau/voice PR #112](https://github.com/joeblau/voice/pull/112)).

The other background limits compound this. Crash logs show an undocumented CPU monitor that kills processes at "80% cpu over 60 seconds", even inside a `BGProcessingTask` ([forums 690666](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/690666)). A "50% cpu over 180 seconds" variant also appears ([forums 123975](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/123975)). Background GPU "won't help on the iPhone (it's not available there)", according to DTS ([forums 807957](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/807957)). whisper.cpp on iOS 26.2 aborts through Metal when backgrounded mid-inference ([whisper.cpp #3531](https://github.com/ggml-org/whisper.cpp/issues/3531)). `BGContinuedProcessingTask` must start "as a result of a person's action, such as tapping a button", has to report progress or be expired, and is cancelled if the user swipes the app away ([BGContinuedProcessingTaskRequest](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/backgroundtasks/bgcontinuedprocessingtaskrequest); [WWDC25 session 227](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/227/)). That makes it a "Process today" button, not an automatic job. One option stands out. **`BGProcessingTaskRequest.requiresExternalPower = true` "will also disable the CPU Monitor feature"** ([requiresExternalPower](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/backgroundtasks/bgprocessingtaskrequest/requiresexternalpower)). The iOS 27.1 SDK header says the system will try to run such a task "within the next two days as long as the user has used your app within the past week" (BGTaskRequest.h, iOS 27.1 SDK), and DTS says these tasks "typically" run overnight ([forums 685525](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/685525)). The charger is where FreeBee gets unconstrained CPU. It gets the ANE there too only if the Background Inference entitlement is granted, because that gate applies "regardless" of task type.

Two system engines may be exempt, although Apple doesn't say so. On macOS 27, speech recognition runs in a separate `localspeechrecognition` XPC service, and on-device Foundation Models inference runs in a system extension. If iOS uses the same layout, SpeechTranscriber's model work wouldn't count against FreeBee's CPU or ANE budget. That would make **SpeechTranscriber the most plausible near-real-time background ASR** on iOS 27 without special entitlements. It is still unproven on a device, and joeblau's fallback is "not verified on a device" ([joeblau/voice PR #121](https://github.com/joeblau/voice/pull/121)). Foundation Models is best-effort in the background: "excessive requests to the model made in the background might be throttled or canceled", and "there's no entitlement or API to influence this" ([forums 833642](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/833642); [forums 833666](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/833666)). In iOS 27, `GenerationError.rateLimited` is deprecated in favour of `LanguageModelError.rateLimited`, which carries an optional `resetDate` and no longer mentions background ([LanguageModelError.rateLimited](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/languagemodelerror/ratelimited(_:))). FreeBee has to handle both error types and keep a persistent queue.

The light path fits comfortably within these limits. Silero processes a 30+ ms chunk in under 1 ms on one CPU thread ([silero-vad](https://github.com/snakers4/silero-vad)). AES-CTR on 4 KB/s, ADTS parsing and AAC decoding are small. Our estimate, not yet measured on an A19 Pro, is **about 1–5% of one core**, more than 15x below either observed CPU-monitor threshold. Storage follows the same logic. Raw ADTS at 32 kbps is **14.4 MB/h, about 230 MB per 16-hour day and about 7 GB per month**. Decoded PCM would be eight times larger. Omi's `diskwrites_resource` report showed a limit of 12.43 KB/s, about 1.07 GB/day ([Omi #11307](https://github.com/BasedHardware/omi/issues/11307)). ADTS at about 4 KB/s uses a third of that, while writing PCM or WAV for later ASR would exceed it. Another earlier contradiction, on archive format, resolves the same way. Store the received ADTS as-is. Re-encoding to an "Opus/AAC archive" adds CPU and generation loss for no gain. The file-protection contradiction is settled too. iOS's default class, `completeUntilFirstUserAuthentication`, stays writable while the phone is locked after the first unlock. Core Data, which SwiftData sits on, defaults its stores to the same class, while `.complete` files "cannot be read from or written to while the device is locked" ([Encrypting your app's files](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/encrypting-your-app-s-files); [NSPersistentStoreFileProtectionKey](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coredata/nspersistentstorefileprotectionkey); [FileProtectionType.complete](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundation/fileprotectiontype/complete)). FreeBee should keep the default class for capture files and the store. The earlier proposal of Data Protection "complete" would break locked-phone capture and overnight processing.

## Parakeet, Silero and pyannote lead on accuracy, but iOS 27 gates their background use

On accuracy, the free on-device leader is **Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3 through FluidAudio**. NVIDIA's model card reports 11.31% WER on AMI, 11.42% on Earnings-22 and 1.93% on LibriSpeech clean, with word-level timestamps across 25 European languages. It degrades gracefully in noise (6.34% average clean, 8.23% at 5 dB SNR) ([NVIDIA card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3)). FluidAudio packages it as a Swift package (Apache-2.0 code; the weights are CC-BY-4.0 and need attribution) with VAD and diarization, which keeps the dependency count at one ([FluidAudio](https://github.com/FluidInference/FluidAudio)). This settles an earlier conflict: the "25 European languages plus Japanese" and "mostly MIT/Apache models" claims came from a secondary summary. Apple's SpeechTranscriber is the zero-download, system-maintained alternative. In the only published comparison it scored 14.0% WER against 11.7% for Parakeet v2 on an Earnings-22 subset. That comparison is a 2025 vendor benchmark run on a Mac ([Argmax](https://www.argmaxinc.com/blog/apple-and-argmax)). iOS 27 added only input helpers such as `AnalyzerInputConverter`, and the result attributes remain time ranges and confidence, with no speaker labels ([Speech updates](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/updates/speech); [ResultAttributeOption](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/speech/speechtranscriber/resultattributeoption)).

No one has published Parakeet's real-time factor on an A19 Pro. The nearest anchor is 181.8 output words/s for Parakeet v3 on a 2018 A12X iPad Pro ([VoicePing benchmark](https://voiceping.net/en/blog/research-offline-speech-transcription-benchmark/)). At conversational pace (about 2.5 words/s) that works out to roughly 70x real time on a much older chip. That conversion is ours, so treat it as an order-of-magnitude figure. It suggests a full day's speech needs minutes, not hours, of foreground or charger compute. Cold-loading the encoder takes 3.4 s on an iPhone 16 Pro Max against 0.16 s warm, so load it once per batch ([FluidAudio Benchmarks](https://github.com/FluidInference/FluidAudio/blob/main/Documentation/Benchmarks.md)). Because iOS 27 charges ANE memory to the app, a resident 445–595 MB encoder in a background process now counts toward the kind of jetsam that killed Omi at about 637 MB. Models should never stay loaded on the BLE path.

For VAD, **Silero v6.2 through FluidAudio's `VadManager` on `.cpuOnly`** runs above 1,000x real time. It returns Silero-style speech intervals with minimum-speech, minimum-silence, padding and maximum-duration controls ([FluidAudio VAD](https://github.com/FluidInference/FluidAudio/blob/main/Documentation/VAD/GettingStarted.md)). Its weakness is noise: it lets through 30.2% false positives on MUSAN noise ([FluidAudio Benchmarks](https://github.com/FluidInference/FluidAudio/blob/main/Documentation/Benchmarks.md)). A second "conversation" layer that merges utterances and drops low-density spans has to sit on top. Apple's SpeechDetector can't take Silero's place. It "only functions in conjunction with a SpeechTranscriber or DictationTranscriber module" ([SpeechDetector](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/speech/speechdetector)), so the earlier suggestion of using it as a standalone VAD doesn't work.

Diarization is the weakest link. Body-worn audio is much harder than meetings. **pyannote community-1 scores 19.9% DER on AMI single-microphone audio but 46.8% on egocentric Ego4D audio** ([pyannote community-1](https://huggingface.co/pyannote/speaker-diarization-community-1)). FluidAudio's port reports 10.6% on AMI under a different scoring protocol, so the two figures aren't comparable. On FluidAudio's own benchmarks, streaming alternatives do worse: Sortformer 26.4%, LS-EEND 20.7% and streaming pyannote 38.2% ([FluidAudio Benchmarks](https://github.com/FluidInference/FluidAudio/blob/main/Documentation/Benchmarks.md)). Two facts settle the live-diarization conflict between the notes. LS-EEND warns that "persistent speaker enrollment may be unreliable", and FluidAudio's `SpeakerManager` works only with the streaming pipeline ([LS-EEND.md](https://github.com/FluidInference/FluidAudio/blob/main/Documentation/Diarization/LS-EEND.md); [SpeakerManager.md](https://github.com/FluidInference/FluidAudio/blob/main/Documentation/Diarization/SpeakerManager.md)). FreeBee should run **community-1 offline once per closed conversation** and use its exclusive output to give each Parakeet word a single speaker. It should then match each cluster's 256-d WeSpeaker centroid by cosine distance against an enrolled wearer voiceprint and a gallery of named people. Argmax's open SDK added centroid embeddings for exactly this kind of cross-session matching ([Argmax OSS releases](https://github.com/argmaxinc/argmax-oss-swift/releases)). The output to rely on is **"Me" versus everyone else**. The wearer is the closest and loudest talker, and that distinction drives which to-dos matter. Speech enhancement should stay out of the ASR path. Two recent studies found that denoising before Whisper and Parakeet *raised* WER in every condition tested ([arXiv 2512.17562](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.17562v1); [arXiv 2603.04710](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04710)). Use it only on a playback copy.

## Apple's 20B on-device model summarizes in 4K-token bites

Apple staff confirmed in August 2026 that **iPhone 17 Pro runs "AFM 3 Core Advanced 20B" on iOS 27.0, and that `SystemLanguageModel.variant` reports which tier is serving** ([forums 843063](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/843063); [Apple ML Research](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/introducing-third-generation-of-apple-foundation-models)). The context window is still small, and the sources conflict. WWDC26 sample code printed `contextSize = 8192` ([WWDC26 session 241](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/241/)), an Apple engineer wrote "4K" in June ([forums 833642](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/833642)), and a measured run on the iOS 27.1 simulator and macOS 27.0 returned 4,096, refusing a 9,060-token prompt ([ampere PR #1021](https://github.com/socket-link/ampere/pull/1021)). FreeBee should read `contextSize` and `variant` at runtime, design for 4K, and treat 8K as a bonus. Custom adapters are gone: the toolkit's "Version 26.0.0 is the last release … not compatible with … iOS 27" ([adapter toolkit](https://developer.apple.com/apple-intelligence/foundation-models-adapter/)). Model updates arrive silently with OS updates and can't be pinned, so a regression set scored with the new Evaluations framework is the only defence ([forums 843063](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/843063)). Guardrails are a real risk for private conversations, and developers report false positives on summarization ([forums 787736](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/787736)).

That shapes the summarization design. **Extract small structured records per chunk, then merge the records rather than the raw text.** Each 1.5–3K-token chunk, cut at speaker-turn boundaries and sized with `tokenCount(for:)`, goes through a fresh session with a small `@Generable` schema: a two-to-four-sentence summary, action items with an owner, people, topics, decisions and quotes. Conversation-level and daily summaries then reduce those JSON records, not the transcript. BooookScore found that hierarchical merging is more coherent and incremental updating keeps more detail ([BooookScore](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.00785)). Apple's own advice for the small window is to delegate "sub-tasks to new sessions" ([forums 833642](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/833642)). For the daily rollup and for guardrail fallbacks, **Qwen3.5-4B at 4-bit on MLX Swift** is the strongest model under 5B. It has a 262K native context and only 8 of its 32 layers use full attention, which keeps the KV cache affordable ([Qwen3.5-4B](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-4B); [Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/qwen3-5-small-models)). Apple's open `MLXLanguageModel` lets it sit behind the same `LanguageModelSession` code ([WWDC26 session 241](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/241/)). It runs on the GPU, so it can only run in the foreground. A single developer measured a per-app memory ceiling of about 6 GB even with the increased-memory entitlement ([forums 834805](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/834805)), and GPU decode loses half its speed within about a minute unplugged ([apple-silicon-llm-bench](https://github.com/john-rocky/apple-silicon-llm-bench)). A "charging and app open" mode is the natural slot for it. FreeBee must never instantiate `PrivateCloudComputeLanguageModel`. iOS 27 lets the framework route to cloud models, and PCC's daily quotas only affect cloud calls anyway ([forums 843046](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/843046)).

Search can start simpler than the earlier notes suggested. At about 300 utterance windows a day, a year is roughly 110K chunks, small enough for SQLite FTS5 BM25 plus speaker and date filters. Embeddings can come in a second step. EmbeddingGemma-300m (768 dimensions with Matryoshka truncation to 128–512, under 200 MB quantized, 100+ languages) is the best small retriever found ([EmbeddingGemma](https://huggingface.co/blog/embeddinggemma)), but no official Core ML or MLX build was confirmed. sqlite-vec is "pre-v1" and doesn't list iOS as a target ([sqlite-vec](https://github.com/asg017/sqlite-vec)). At this scale, brute-force cosine search over int8 vectors in Swift avoids that dependency; this is our inference. Apple's alternative is to donate App Entities to the Spotlight semantic index and query it with `SpotlightSearchTool` ([WWDC26 session 246](https://developer.apple.com/wwdc26/246/)). iOS 27 has a known issue where an unconfigured tool overflows the on-device model's token limit ([iOS 27 release notes](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/ios-ipados-release-notes/ios-ipados-27-release-notes)), and no source confirms that the index stays on the device. It is a secondary surface for Siri, not the system of record. "What did Sam say about X last week?" then becomes a `@Generable` query parse, a filtered hybrid retrieval, and an answer that cites chunk IDs and audio offsets.

## Reviewers rewarded fewer outputs and a mute that works

The official Bee app's structure was sound: a daily memory, a conversation list with takeaways and speakers, to-dos, "Facts", and the "Buzz" chat. Its failures were about volume and precision. A month-long reviewer had to verify **25+ dubious "facts" a day** ("apparently I want a puppy?"), and true-crime podcasts were treated as real conversations ([mikecann.blog](https://mikecann.blog/posts/always-listening-a-month-with-the-bee-computer-ai-assistant)). Forbes found Bee suggested "dozens" of to-dos a day, many pulled from TV ([Forbes Vetted](https://www.forbes.com/sites/forbes-personal-shopper/article/best-ai-wearables/)). A 2026 review called the daily recaps of commitments "surprisingly useful" and the generic summaries hit-or-miss ([Big Guy on Stuff](https://bigguyonstuff.com/ai-wearables-2026-honest-review/)). Omi closes a conversation after a user-adjustable silence timeout ([Omi help](https://help.omi.me/en/articles/13153774-advanced-settings-settings-screen)). Plaud records only on intent. Limitless pitched a "Consent Mode" that captures only voices of people who agreed ([Business Insider](https://WWW.BUSINESSINSIDER.IN/limitless-ai-pendant-clips-clothes-hears-records-everything-2024-4)). Friend's mic that couldn't be muted earned "nothing at all to recommend it" ([Boing Boing on Wired](https://boingboing.net/2025/09/08/ai-powered-eavesdropping-gadgets-reviewed.html)). Business risk points the same way. Amazon now sells Bee Pro at $11.99/month, and its free tier keeps only 14 days of memory ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/15/amazons-bee-launches-two-new-personal-ai-devices-starting-at-75/)). Meta shut down Limitless pendant sales and Rewind capture after acquiring the company ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2025/12/05/rewind-limitless-meta-acquisition/)). A local-only app with unlimited retention, replayable audio and open export is a real differentiator, not only a privacy stance.

These lessons translate into specific mechanisms. To-dos and facts go into a single **review inbox**, capped per day, with each item linked to its quote and timestamp. To-dos are drawn only from speech by the wearer or addressed to them. Facts need evidence from at least two conversations. Segments that look like media are flagged and excluded from extraction. Two signals feed that flag: a sound classifier, and the heuristic "continuous speech with no enrolled speaker", which avoids jkmills' admittedly "coarse" PANNs tagger ([jkmills README](https://github.com/jkmills/bee-wearable-ble/blob/main/README.md)). The evening digest leads with commitments. Mute is reachable from the Bee's double press, a Live Activity, a Control Center control and Shortcuts. The Live Activity also acts as the recording light, because BLE audio triggers no iOS microphone indicator. The law matters here. App Review 2.5.14 requires "a clear visual and/or audible indication when recording" ([App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). About 11 US states, including California, Florida, Illinois and Washington, require all-party consent ([Layer3 Labs](https://www.layer3labs.io/guides/two-party-consent-states)). An opt-in Consent Mode that transcribes only enrolled voices is the strongest local answer. This is not legal advice.

## Recommended FreeBee architecture

| Layer | Choice | Runs when and where | Key rules |
|---|---|---|---|
| Link | AccessorySetupKit pairing, CoreBluetooth, `bluetooth-central` only, constant restore ID, pending `connect()` immediately on every drop | Always | No `audio` keep-alive; RSSI poll foreground-only (once a minute in background only during the disconnect study); back off command retries, never the pending connect |
| Session | `SetEncryptionKey(real SKV1 token)` → `GetSessionNonce` → subscribe audio → `SetDeviceState(1)` if the user wants capture → confirm via `GetDeviceState` or `A003` | Every connection | Treat `0x8004` as success; session is ready only when decrypted bytes start `0xFFF1`; watchdog re-arms if subscribed but no valid frames arrive for N s; A/B test the official minimal sequence |
| Decrypt | CommonCrypto `kCCModeCTR` with `kCCModeOptionCTR_BE`, 16-byte IV = nonce‖seq (big-endian) | BLE wake | System framework, no BoringSSL; swift-crypto `AES._CTR` is the alternative ([swift-crypto](https://github.com/apple/swift-crypto/blob/main/Sources/CryptoExtras/AES/AES_CTR.swift)) |
| Framing | Port the Mac `AdtsReassembler`: syncword, fixed-header invariants (AAC-LC, 16 kHz, mono, no CRC), `frame_length` > 7, next sync at +length, 1-byte resync; drop the first 3 frames per new nonce | BLE wake | Never assume 495-byte packets; count bad frames and lost packets separately |
| Archive | Append validated ADTS unchanged to 15-minute segment files; SwiftData index of nonce, seq range, wall-clock start, frame count and gaps | BLE wake | No PCM, no re-encode; batched flushes; default `completeUntilFirstUserAuthentication`; exclude from backup |
| Live layer (optional, after measurement) | AAC→Float32 16 kHz via `AVAudioConverter`; Silero `.cpuOnly`; conversation open/close events | BLE wake | Kill switch if MetricKit shows CPU or memory terminations |
| ASR, final | FluidAudio Parakeet TDT v3, word timestamps; CTC-110M custom vocabulary later | Foreground (ANE); nightly on charger (ANE if entitlement granted, otherwise CPU with monitor off) | Load once per batch; resumable per segment; CC-BY attribution in About |
| ASR, draft and fallback | SpeechTranscriber through `AnalyzerInputConverter` | Background drafts only if the probe passes; other locales | Check `AssetInventory` status before each session |
| Diarization and ID | pyannote community-1 offline (FluidAudio), exclusive output, per closed conversation; WeSpeaker centroids against an enrolled "Me" and a named gallery | Foreground or nightly | Enroll through the Bee itself; running-mean profiles; tune cosine threshold from ~0.65 |
| Understanding | Foundation Models (`variant`, `contextSize` read at runtime), `@Generable` map per chunk in fresh sessions, reduce per conversation and per day | Map best-effort after each conversation; reduce nightly | Handle both `rateLimited` types, guardrail and context errors; persistent queue; never PCC |
| Long-context LLM | Qwen3.5-4B 4-bit via MLX Swift behind `MLXLanguageModel` | Foreground only ("Process today", or charging with app open) | Cap GPU cache; checkpoint before backgrounding |
| Search | FTS5 BM25 + speaker/date filters, then EmbeddingGemma vectors + reciprocal-rank fusion; FM query parser and cited answers | Index nightly; query in foreground | Separate SQLite file keyed by chunk UUID; Spotlight entities as a secondary surface |
| Telemetry | Compact per-packet log, link-event timings, launch reasons, `MetricManager` reports | Always | Defines "% of day captured" |

**Background strategy.** Compute runs in four tiers, each matched to what iOS 27 permits. The *BLE wake path* does only receive, decrypt, validate, append and index, and later, if measurements allow, CPU VAD. *Foreground* sessions catch up ASR and diarization on the ANE and serve search and chat. *"Process today"* is a `BGContinuedProcessingTask` with honest progress reporting (segments done out of total), started by a button. *Nightly* work is a `BGProcessingTask` with `requiresExternalPower = true` that runs ASR, diarization, reduce and daily summaries, embeddings and retention cleanup. Every stage checkpoints per segment. The Bee's `A005` charging event is a natural "day is over" signal: no new audio can arrive, so FreeBee can close open conversations and schedule the heavy pass. FreeBee should request the Background Inference entitlement and test it inside both a continued task and a processing task. If it's unavailable to a personal team, the nightly pass runs on CPU with the monitor off.

**Storage.** Expect about 230 MB of ADTS per 16-hour day. The default retention keeps transcripts, extracts and speech audio for a user-set number of days, deletes non-speech audio once it has been transcribed, and lets the user star clips to keep indefinitely. The SwiftData store and its `-wal`/`-shm` files stay in the default protection class. The SKV1 token stays in the Keychain under `AfterFirstUnlockThisDeviceOnly`, as it is today.

**Privacy.** Run with zero network once set up. Stage FluidAudio models locally, which its VAD docs support through manual model paths ([FluidAudio VAD](https://github.com/FluidInference/FluidAudio/blob/main/Documentation/VAD/GettingStarted.md)), rather than downloading them from Hugging Face at runtime. Pre-install SpeechTranscriber assets once. Never touch PCC, and audit the result with iOS's App Privacy Report. Add a Face ID lock on the UI, Consent Mode, quick mute everywhere, a visible recording state, full Markdown/JSON plus audio export, and "delete everything".

## Risks and open questions

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| OTA update or official-app re-pairing invalidates the SKV1 token or changes encryption | Capture stops entirely | Keep the official app deleted, never accept updates, back up the token; 0.5.0 slot as a last resort only |

| Bystander recording law | Legal exposure | Consent Mode, mute, indicator, local only |

## Conclusion

The bottleneck has moved. A year ago the open question for a local wearable pipeline was whether a phone could transcribe and diarize well enough. In October 2026 the models are good enough, and the deciding factors are **scheduling policy and radio continuity**: which iOS state is allowed to touch the Neural Engine, and how many seconds pass between a link drop and the next valid ADTS frame. That changes what FreeBee is during the day. It is a disciplined recorder that captures reliably and processes later, and the charger becomes the real AI platform. One practical consequence is that FreeBee's value at 9 p.m. depends on how complete the archive is, not on how much was computed live. Gap telemetry and reconnect logic deserve more care than model selection.

The second implication is strategic. Amazon's Bee discards audio, keeps 14 days of memory on its free tier and routes everything through its servers. A local pipeline that keeps audio linked to every quote, stores history without limit and exports openly is not a privacy-flavoured imitation of Bee; it's a product Bee can't offer. Several architectural forks are still open: whether to run live SpeechTranscriber drafts, whether to sleep the Bee in its VAD states, whether to rely on the ANE overnight, and how to sequence reconnects. Each one comes down to a day of measurement on the user's own iPhone and Bee, so the ingest path and its telemetry are the highest-leverage code to write next.
