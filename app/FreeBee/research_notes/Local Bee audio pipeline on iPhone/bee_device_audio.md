# Bee Pioneer (legacy 0xBEEE, firmware 0.6.x): audio capture capabilities and constraints for streaming to our own iPhone app

Notes as of 2026-10-20. Labels used throughout:
- **[HW-ours]**: measured on our own Bee (legacy 0xBEEE, fw 0.6.1) from our capture `captures/20261020-201740-raw.jsonl` / `.aac` (analysed this session with a Python script and ffprobe/ffmpeg) or from our `docs/PROTOCOL.md`.
- **[HW-jkmills]**: confirmed on jkmills' physical unit (legacy hardware, reported fw 0.6.5).
- **[Static]**: read from decompiled official Android app code (`com.bee.android` 1.0.3), not observed live.
- **[Vendor]**: Bee/Amazon marketing or FAQ claims, not independently tested.
- **[Reported]**: third-party reviews/users.

Local source files: [our PROTOCOL.md](../../../../docs/PROTOCOL.md), [our IOS_PLAN.md](../../../../docs/IOS_PLAN.md), [our audio.py](../../../../mac/src/beekit/audio.py), [our transcribe.py](../../../../mac/src/beekit/transcribe.py), our capture (`captures/20261020-201740-raw.jsonl`).

## 1. Hardware: microphones, SoC/BLE chip, flash, battery capacity, advertised battery life

### Takeaway
Bee's official figures are "dual microphones" and "up to 7 days / 160 hours" of battery. No FCC filing, teardown, SoC name, flash size or mAh rating could be found. The firmware fingerprint (MCUboot + Zephyr MCUmgr SMP, ATT MTU 498, SMP netbuf 2475) strongly suggests a Nordic nRF Connect SDK (Zephyr) build, but the exact chip (for example nRF5340) is unconfirmed. Nobody has published a measured battery life under continuous BLE streaming.

### Cited Findings
- [Vendor] Bee Frontier (the Oct 2026 successor) is described as having "Dual microphones and advanced noise filtering"; its FAQ says "Bee uses an advanced dual-microphone system with sophisticated noise filtering" — [bee.computer/products/frontier](https://bee.computer/products/frontier). Reviews of the Pioneer also say two microphones, though they disagree on whether these are "dual beamforming mics" or "two microphones paired with advanced noise filtering" — [ticnote review](https://ticnote.com/en/blog/bee-wearable-ai-review); [thisismytech](https://www.thisismytech.com/home/2025/8/22/the-bee-pioneer-edition-a-new-take-on-what-a-wearable-can-do) (search-snippet level, not fetched in full).
- [Vendor] Bee claimed an "industry-first 7-day battery life (160+ hours)" for the Bee Pioneer — [bee-computer.notion.site (search snippet; page did not render)](https://bee-computer.notion.site/features-capabilities). For Frontier the FAQ says "up to 7 day battery life (up to 160 hours)", and the homepage says "7 days of battery" — [Frontier page](https://bee.computer/products/frontier); [bee.computer](https://bee.computer/).
- [Vendor] Frontier FAQ: "By default, the device microphones will turn off after a certain time period of silence. You can change this timing by selecting from a list of options ranging from 5 minutes to 24 hours." — [Frontier page](https://bee.computer/products/frontier).
- [Reported] One App Store reviewer (Nov 30, 2025) says: "I can get three long days of work before I need to recharge using the convenient USB-C socket." — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/bee-your-personal-ai/id6480349491).
- [Reported, competitor source] UMEVO, which sells a competing recorder, says the 7-day figure applies to standby and that active listening gives "1.5 to 2 days" — [UMEVO review](https://www.umevo.ai/blogs/ume-all-posts/bee-ai-pendant-complete-review-features-battery-life-and-pricing-2026). Treat this with caution because of the conflict of interest. No methodology is given.
- [Reported] Pioneer list price is $49.99. The device is pill-shaped (about 1.8 x 0.8 in, per Forbes), charges over USB-C, and has an LED and one multifunction button — [search summary citing Forbes Vetted](https://www.forbes.com/sites/forbes-personal-shopper/article/best-ai-wearables/); [bestaiwearables](https://www.bestaiwearables.com/wearables/bee-your-new-wearable-personal-ai).
- [HW-jkmills] Over USB, a Mac sees no mass-storage, serial or USB device entry: "it appears to be charging-only" — [jkmills PROTOCOL.md](https://github.com/jkmills/bee-wearable-ble/blob/main/PROTOCOL.md).
- [HW-ours] The firmware exposes the standard MCUmgr SMP service `8d53dc1d-…`. The bootloader reports `MCUboot`. The image list shows slot 0 = 0.6.1 (active, confirmed) and slot 1 = 0.5.0 (bootable). SMP params are `buf_size 2475, buf_count 4`. MTU negotiated on macOS is 498 — [our PROTOCOL.md](../../../../docs/PROTOCOL.md).
- Nordic's own samples use `CONFIG_MCUMGR_TRANSPORT_NETBUF_SIZE` = 2475, and the netbuf must exceed the BLE MTU — [Nordic DevZone "BLE MTU size" (nRF5340/NCS thread)](https://devzone.nordicsemi.com/f/nordic-q-a/127056/ble-mtu-size); [Zephyr MCUmgr transport Kconfig](https://codex.ac6.fr/Zephyr/xref/Zephyr-4.1.0/subsys/mgmt/mcumgr/transport/Kconfig).
- A search of fccid.io for "Bee Computer" returned no Bee Computer grantee or Bee wearable FCC ID. The only hits were unrelated "Bee" products — [fccid.io search](https://fccid.io/search.php?q=Bee+Computer). Searches for a teardown also found nothing.
- [Static] The official app treats hardware revisions 3 and 4 as "Frontier" (`BatteryLevelCalculator.isFrontier`). Frontier reports battery as LE uint16 millivolts plus a charging byte, while legacy units report `[percent, charging]` — [jkmills PROTOCOL.md](https://github.com/jkmills/bee-wearable-ble/blob/main/PROTOCOL.md). Omi's connector has a LiPo mV→% table for the 3-byte format (for example 4180 mV→85%, 3480 mV→0%) — [Omi bee_connection.dart](https://github.com/BasedHardware/omi/blob/main/app/lib/services/devices/connectors/bee_connection.dart).

### Inferences
- The SMP service UUID, MCUboot with dual image slots, a 498-byte ATT MTU (the usual Zephyr `BT_L2CAP_TX_MTU=498` for 251-byte data-length extension) and netbuf 2475 (Nordic's sample value) together point strongly to Zephyr/nRF Connect SDK on a Nordic nRF52- or nRF53-series SoC. A Zephyr build on another vendor's chip can't be ruled out. "nRF5340" specifically is speculation.
- Two MCUboot image slots mean the flash holds at least two full firmware images. Nothing indicates a separate audio-storage partition.
- The 7-day claim almost certainly assumes the default silence auto-off (Frontier FAQ) and intermittent capture, not 24 h of continuous 32 kbps streaming. Expect continuous streaming to last far less. The only data points are the "three long days of work" user report and the competitor's 1.5–2 days, both anecdotal.

### Gaps
- No FCC ID, teardown, chip marking, flash size or battery capacity (mAh) found. FCC filings may sit under a parent/ODM grantee name that wasn't searched (Bluush Inc is the App Store developer name). Searching the FCC OET database for "Bluush" is the next step.
- No measured battery life under continuous streaming at 32 kbps or 8 kbps. This needs our own test: stream continuously and log `A004` battery events and `GetBattery` every 10 min.
- Whether the two mics are beamformed on-device before encoding is unknown. The stream is mono (see Q4).

## 2. Device states 2 (sleep, HW VAD) and 4 (sleep, SW VAD), and how the official app consumed audio

### Takeaway
States 2 and 4 are real firmware states. They can be set without a session, read back correctly, and keep the BLE link up. Nobody has published what the device does in them, for example whether it wakes on speech, emits an `A003` event and starts streaming. The official app streams live: each ADTS frame goes over a Socket.IO WebSocket as it arrives, with server-side STT. It does not batch-upload, and it has no on-device archive. On our unit, the RECORDING state (1) streamed continuously, silence included, so any VAD gating has to happen on the phone.

### Cited Findings
- [Static] `DeviceState` enum: 0 MUTE, 1 RECORDING, 2 SLEEP_HW_VAD ("sleep, hardware voice-activity-detection wake"), 3 POWER_OFF, 4 SLEEP_SW_VAD ("sleep, software voice-activity-detection wake"). The only named factories are `powerOff()`, `sleepHwVad()` and `sleepSwVad()`. Mute and recording come from a boolean constructor used by `BeeProtocolMutePlugin` — [jkmills PROTOCOL.md](https://github.com/jkmills/bee-wearable-ble/blob/main/PROTOCOL.md).
- [HW-jkmills] "`sleep hw-vad` also confirmed working: sending SetDeviceState(SLEEP_HW_VAD) kept the connection alive (unlike POWER_OFF …) and a subsequent GetDeviceState read back SLEEP_HW_VAD". `sleep sw-vad` also read back correctly. Both work without an encryption session. Mute and recording only stick inside a session — [jkmills PROTOCOL.md](https://github.com/jkmills/bee-wearable-ble/blob/main/PROTOCOL.md); [jkmills README](https://github.com/jkmills/bee-wearable-ble/blob/main/README.md). Our PROTOCOL.md says the same about sessions ("Mute and recording only stick inside an open session; the others work without one") — [our PROTOCOL.md](../../../../docs/PROTOCOL.md).
- [Static] The stock app's UI exposes only brightness and power-off. Sleep modes, TX power and bitrate are wired into the protocol but not offered to users. `BeeProtocolPowerSavingPlugin`, `BeeProtocolSessionTimeoutPlugin` and `BeeProtocolUtterancePlugin` ("higher-level session/power policy") were not decompiled — [jkmills PROTOCOL.md](https://github.com/jkmills/bee-wearable-ble/blob/main/PROTOCOL.md).
- [HW-jkmills, live HCI capture of the official Android app] On each of 7 reconnects the app sent: GetDeviceID → GetSecureIds → GetDeviceState → GetFirmwareVersion → GetBattery → GetBitrate → GetTxLevel → **SetDeviceState(MUTE)** → GetSessionNonce → enable the audio CCCD. SetEncryptionKey is sent only at pairing — [jkmills PROTOCOL.md](https://github.com/jkmills/bee-wearable-ble/blob/main/PROTOCOL.md).
- [HW-jkmills] The physical button is the real recording toggle: "a single physical button press toggles the device's own recording LED on/off, and audio streaming genuinely starts/stops with it". With a session open, the device pushes `A003` (`0101` = RECORDING/AAC, `0001` = MUTE/AAC) on each toggle — [jkmills PROTOCOL.md](https://github.com/jkmills/bee-wearable-ble/blob/main/PROTOCOL.md).
- [Static] `BeeProtocolButtonEventsPlugin` sends no BLE command back. It relays `onpress`, `onrelease` and `ondoublepress` to the backend over Socket.IO, and plays a memo sound when hold is set to "memo" — [jkmills PROTOCOL.md](https://github.com/jkmills/bee-wearable-ble/blob/main/PROTOCOL.md).
- [Static] Official audio path: BLE notify → `BeeProtocolAudioStreamPlugin.onAudioMessage` (gated on muted, codec and readiness) → `decryptPacket` → `AudioMessageDecoder.processPacket` (ADTS framing, 3-frame warm-up discard) → `BeeStreamer.sendAudio(frame, deviceName, captureId, codec)`. That emits Socket.IO `"audio_data"` (plus an `"O"` argument for Opus) over a WebSocket. The server returns `new_utterance_partial` and `new_utterance`. "Transcription is server-side, not on-device and not in local app code" — [jkmills PROTOCOL.md](https://github.com/jkmills/bee-wearable-ble/blob/main/PROTOCOL.md).
- [Static] The official app bundles a local **Silero VAD** model (`sileroModelBytes`, `com.bee.background.audio.AacVadRmsDecoder`) "used only for a local voice-energy/RMS meter" (the `onVoiceEnergy` callback) — [jkmills PROTOCOL.md](https://github.com/jkmills/bee-wearable-ble/blob/main/PROTOCOL.md).
- [HW-jkmills, author's characterisation] The app "has no client-side 'record for N seconds' timer … packets flow whenever the device itself decides to send them (unmuted + voice detected), for as long as the BLE connection stays open; the app relays whatever arrives to its backend live" — [jkmills README](https://github.com/jkmills/bee-wearable-ble/blob/main/README.md). The "voice detected" part is not backed by any test in the repo.
- [HW-ours] Our 45 s capture in RECORDING state: 366 notifications over 44.82 s gave 698 ADTS frames = 44.67 s of audio at 1024 samples/frame, so the stream was gap-free. ffmpeg RMS per window shows quiet stretches around −37 to −43 dBFS streamed alongside speech around −15 to −25 dBFS. The largest inter-notification gap was 210 ms — our capture (`captures/20261020-201740-raw.jsonl`). In state 1 the firmware did not suppress silence.
- [Reported, Frida hook on the official Android app, Oct 2026] A 30 s capture at the `BeeStreamer.sendAudio` boundary gave 468 frames / 29,904 bytes = 29.952 s, also continuous — [Omi issue #20828](https://github.com/BasedHardware/omi/issues/20828).
- [Vendor] Frontier mics "turn off after a certain time period of silence", configurable from 5 minutes to 24 hours — [Frontier page](https://bee.computer/products/frontier). Press-and-hold is a voice note, double press a bookmark, and the green LED means capturing — [Frontier page](https://bee.computer/products/frontier); [TechCrunch hands-on, Jan 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/12/hands-on-with-bee-amazons-latest-ai-wearable/).

### Inferences
- The "silence auto-off after 5 min–24 h" setting is most plausibly the app or firmware moving the device out of RECORDING after a stretch of silence, possibly into state 2 or 4 so voice can wake it. Since `PowerSavingPlugin` and `SessionTimeoutPlugin` weren't decompiled, this is unconfirmed. If states 2/4 wake on voice and return to RECORDING (with `A003`), our app could rely on them as a low-power always-on mode. If not, the app would have to manage the timeout itself.
- "Hardware VAD" probably means a low-power sound or voice detector in the mic or audio front end that wakes the MCU, and "software VAD" a detector running on the MCU. HW VAD would likely draw less power but trigger less precisely. This rests on the names alone.
- Streaming is continuous in state 1, so the phone carries the full 32 kbps around the clock. Our on-phone VAD (Silero, or Apple SpeechAnalyzer/SpeechDetector) has to drop silence before transcription. That is what the official app's bundled Silero VAD suggests it did for its meter.
- The official app mutes on every connect and the button toggles recording on the device. A local-only app should therefore treat `A003` as the source of truth for recording state, not its own last command.

### Gaps
- Whether states 2/4 ever wake on their own and start streaming audio (and whether they then emit `A003 [1, codec]`) is not documented anywhere. Proposed experiment on our unit: open a session, subscribe to audio, SetDeviceState(2), stay silent 30 s, then talk. Log `A003` events and audio packets, then repeat with state 4. Also measure battery drain per state.
- What exactly the official app's silence-timeout logic sends over BLE is unknown. A PacketLogger trace of the official iOS app during a long silent stretch would show it.
- What the official iOS app does is undocumented. All decompilation is of the Android app.

## 3. On-device storage when the phone is away, and any download protocol

### Takeaway
No evidence of any on-device audio archive or download protocol. The command set has no list or fetch command, USB is charge-only, and Bee says audio is never stored. Audio the Bee produces while it has no phone connection should be assumed lost. Our app has to stay connected to capture anything.

### Cited Findings
- [Static] "No 'list/fetch stored recordings' or file-transfer command was found among the BeeRequest commands." The full command list is C001–C01D (TX power, firmware version, device state, bitrate, device ID, battery, brightness, encryption key, nonce, secure IDs, DSN, charging speed) — [jkmills PROTOCOL.md](https://github.com/jkmills/bee-wearable-ble/blob/main/PROTOCOL.md). jkmills' best explanation is "local firmware state/buffering with a limited window, flushed live … not a persistent on-device archive synced later. This remains unconfirmed."
- [HW-jkmills] USB shows no mass storage, serial or USB device, so it looks charging-only — [jkmills PROTOCOL.md](https://github.com/jkmills/bee-wearable-ble/blob/main/PROTOCOL.md).
- [Vendor] "Bee does not store your audio—conversations are processed in real-time and then discarded." — [Bee blog, Sept 23 2026](https://bee.computer/blog/private-ai). "Bee processes conversations in real-time and no audio is ever stored." — [Bee blog, Jan 5 2026](https://bee.computer/blog/building-bee-at-amazon-how-the-wearable-ai-device-is-evolving). The TechCrunch hands-on says it "discards the audio after transcription" — [TechCrunch, Jan 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/12/hands-on-with-bee-amazons-latest-ai-wearable/).
- [Reported] A reviewer: "Bee doesn't deliver an audio recording, only a transcription." Another: "The pioneer disconnects ALL THE TIME if Bee isn't in the background/foreground apps" — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/bee-your-personal-ai/id6480349491). A comparison article says Bee is not the right choice for buyers who need guaranteed offline operation (competitor-authored) — [UMEVO comparison](https://www.umevo.ai/blogs/ume-all-posts/ultra-compact-recorders-notta-memo-vs-bee-pioneer-vs-plaud-notepin).
- [HW-ours] Audio sequence numbers start at `0x00000000` in our capture (first packet seq 0, last 0x16D = 365 of 366) — our capture (`captures/20261020-201740-raw.jsonl`). The SMP service exists, but we only send read ops. OS app info returns ENOTSUP — [our PROTOCOL.md](../../../../docs/PROTOCOL.md).

### Inferences
- The sequence counter starts at 0 for each session or subscription, so it can't reveal audio missed while disconnected. It can only reveal drops within a connection.
- There is no store-and-forward, so captures survive only while BLE is connected and the app is alive. The app must reconnect quickly (state restoration plus pending connect), and audio during any gap is lost. The app should log gaps as "not captured" in the timeline.
- Zephyr MCUmgr has a filesystem group. If the firmware enabled it, SMP fs reads could reveal files, but nothing suggests audio is written to flash. Probing it is low value and should stay read-only.

### Gaps
- The device's internal RAM buffer depth (how many seconds it holds when the link stalls briefly) is unknown. It could be measured by briefly blocking the central (for example a 2–5 s stall) and checking whether sequence numbers jump or late packets arrive in bursts.
- No list or fetch command was searched for in firmware. The firmware image isn't available, though it would appear in a PacketLogger trace if the official app pushed an update.

## 4. Codecs (0 Opus, 1 AAC), SetBitrate values, sample rate, channels, trade-offs

### Takeaway
On our unit the stream is MPEG-4 AAC-LC in ADTS, 16 kHz, mono, no CRC, about 32 kbps (GetBitrate = 32000), 1024 samples (64 ms) per frame, packed into fixed 495-byte notifications. jkmills' unit on fw 0.6.5 reported 8000 bps, and a Frida capture from the official app measured about 8 kbps, so the bitrate varies between units and firmware versions. No command to switch codec exists. Opus (0) is an enum value that nobody has seen on a real device. Which SetBitrate values are valid has never been tested.

### Cited Findings
- [HW-ours] ADTS headers in all 698 frames: MPEG-4, profile = AAC-LC, sampling index 8 = 16,000 Hz, channel config 1 (mono), protection_absent = 1 (no CRC), 1 raw data block per frame. Frame length is 213–345 bytes (median 254, mean 255.8). Computed bitrate is 31.98 kbps, and ffprobe reports "aac (LC), 16000 Hz, mono, fltp, 31 kb/s", duration 44.73 s — our capture .aac (`captures/20261020-201740.aac`). GetBitrate = 32000 and codec = 1 (AAC) — [our PROTOCOL.md](../../../../docs/PROTOCOL.md).
- [HW-ours] Every audio notification is exactly 495 bytes (4-byte seq + 491 bytes ciphertext) at MTU 498. The over-the-air payload rate is 4,042 B/s ≈ 32.3 kbps — our capture (`captures/20261020-201740-raw.jsonl`).
- [HW-jkmills] On fw 0.6.5, `GetBitrate` returned 8000. Real decrypted audio was "64 ADTS frames, every single one reporting exactly 16000Hz", frame sizes 57–115 bytes, first bytes `fff160400d02…` (AAC-LC, 16 kHz, mono) — [jkmills PROTOCOL.md](https://github.com/jkmills/bee-wearable-ble/blob/main/PROTOCOL.md).
- [Reported] Frida capture inside the official Android app: AAC-LC ADTS, 16 kHz mono, 468 frames / 29,904 bytes over 29.952 s, about 64 B/frame ≈ 8.0 kbps — [Omi issue #20828](https://github.com/BasedHardware/omi/issues/20828).
- [Static] `SetBitrate` is C008 with an LE int32 payload. `GetBitrate` is C009 and returns an LE int32. No codec-set command exists. Codec appears only as byte 1 of `GetDeviceState` and the `A003` event (`0` OPUS, `1` AAC, other = UNKNOWN). The official app sends `GetBitrate` on every connect but never `SetBitrate` in the captured traffic — [jkmills PROTOCOL.md](https://github.com/jkmills/bee-wearable-ble/blob/main/PROTOCOL.md). jkmills: "`tx-power` and `bitrate` writes are implemented but untested live" — [jkmills README](https://github.com/jkmills/bee-wearable-ble/blob/main/README.md).
- [Static] The official app branches on codec only when uploading (`"audio_data"` plus an `"O"` flag for Opus). Opus local framing in `AudioMessageDecoder`/`AudioSessionManager` "wasn't traced" — [jkmills PROTOCOL.md](https://github.com/jkmills/bee-wearable-ble/blob/main/PROTOCOL.md).
- [Static/HW] The official decoder discards the first 3 ADTS frames per stream (`adtsWarmup = 3`) and stops scanning on `frameLen <= 7` — [jkmills PROTOCOL.md](https://github.com/jkmills/bee-wearable-ble/blob/main/PROTOCOL.md). Omi's desktop connector treats `<7` as a false sync and advances one byte, to avoid a permanent stall after a dropped notification — [Omi BeeDeviceConnection.swift](https://github.com/BasedHardware/omi/blob/main/desktop/macos/Desktop/Sources/Bluetooth/Connections/BeeDeviceConnection.swift).
- Our Swift settings screen already warns that "A wrong bitrate could affect audio recorded by the Bee" and puts bitrate behind an advanced screen — [SettingsView.swift](../../../../app/FreeBee/FreeBee/Views/SettingsView.swift); [our IOS_PLAN.md](../../../../docs/IOS_PLAN.md).

### Inferences
- Data volume, computed from the measured rates: 32 kbps = 4.0 KB/s = 14.4 MB/h, about 230 MB per 16 h day. 8 kbps is about 3.6 MB/h. Storing the compressed ADTS on the iPhone is cheap at either rate. Decoded 16 kHz/16-bit PCM is 256 kbps (115 MB/h), so decode just in time and don't keep PCM.
- ADTS header overhead is 7 B per 64 ms frame, about 0.9 kbps (about 2.7% at 32 kbps, about 11% at 8 kbps).
- At 32 kbps each 491-byte packet holds about 1.9 frames, so frames straddle packets and one lost notification corrupts about 2–3 frames (about 130–190 ms). The reassembler must resync, which ours and Omi's do. At 8 kbps a packet holds about 7–8 frames, so notifications arrive about every 0.5 s and a loss costs about 0.5 s.
- For ASR, 16 kHz mono AAC-LC at 32 kbps is near-transparent for speech and matches Whisper/SpeechAnalyzer's native 16 kHz input, so no resampling is needed. 8 kbps AAC-LC at 16 kHz is quite lossy, with high-frequency cutoff and artifacts, and should degrade WER and diarization embeddings somewhat. Keep our unit at 32000 and don't call SetBitrate unless deliberately experimenting.
- The difference between 0.6.1 (32000) and 0.6.5 (8000) suggests later firmware or the backend lowered the default bitrate, perhaps for battery or bandwidth. This is unproven, since jkmills' unit could have been set differently.
- Opus would need a decoder on iOS. AVAudioConverter can decode Opus in recent iOS versions, but that wasn't verified this session. With no codec-select command it is moot for us.

### Gaps
- Valid SetBitrate range and steps, and whether the firmware applies a change live or after reconnect, are all untested. A careful test would be GetBitrate → SetBitrate(16000) → check ADTS frame sizes → SetBitrate(32000) to restore.
- Whether the Pioneer firmware can produce Opus at all is unknown.
- AAC encoder delay/priming and why the app drops 3 frames (warm-up of the encoder or the decryptor) aren't documented. 3 frames is 192 ms.

## 5. jkmills' transcribe.py, diarize.py, speakers.py, audio_tagging.py: what they do and reported results

### Takeaway
jkmills' pipeline is a laptop-class Python stack: faster-whisper "base" with built-in VAD, pyannote speaker-diarization-3.1, cosine-matched persistent speaker profiles, and PANNs CNN14 media tagging. It is reported as "confirmed working end-to-end", but only on "one short single-speaker clip". No WER, DER or speaker-ID accuracy figures were reported.

### Cited Findings
- `transcribe.py`: `faster_whisper.WhisperModel(model_size="base", device="auto", compute_type="auto")`, `model.transcribe(path, language=None, vad_filter=True)`. It returns (start, end, text) segments. The docstring calls "base" "a reasonable default for short voice clips on a laptop CPU" — [transcribe.py](https://github.com/jkmills/bee-wearable-ble/blob/main/src/bee_control/transcribe.py).
- `diarize.py`: `pyannote.audio.Pipeline.from_pretrained("pyannote/speaker-diarization-3.1")`, which needs a Hugging Face account, gated-model acceptance for speaker-diarization-3.1 and segmentation-3.0, an `hf auth login` token, and system FFmpeg for torchcodec. It returns per-turn labels plus one embedding per speaker from `output.speaker_embeddings`. Transcript segments are merged to speakers by maximum time overlap — [diarize.py](https://github.com/jkmills/bee-wearable-ble/blob/main/src/bee_control/diarize.py).
- `speakers.py`: `speaker_profiles.json` holds one embedding per named person (gitignored as biometric data). Matching uses cosine similarity with `DEFAULT_MATCH_THRESHOLD = 0.6` ("not independently tuned here"). Enrolment for an existing name sets `profile = (old + new) / 2`, and `process_audio.py` prompts interactively to name unknown speakers — [speakers.py](https://github.com/jkmills/bee-wearable-ble/blob/main/src/bee_control/speakers.py); [process_audio.py](https://github.com/jkmills/bee-wearable-ble/blob/main/process_audio.py).
- `audio_tagging.py`: PANNs CNN14 (AudioSet, 527 classes) via `panns_inference` on CPU at 32 kHz, top-5 labels. A segment is flagged as media if a tag ≥ 0.15 matches {Television, Radio, Music, Theme music, Background music, Film, Broadcast}. An optional sliding window is 2 s with a 1 s hop. The author warns it's "a coarse heuristic, not a reliable classifier" that can't tell a person in the room from a person on TV — [audio_tagging.py](https://github.com/jkmills/bee-wearable-ble/blob/main/src/bee_control/audio_tagging.py); [README](https://github.com/jkmills/bee-wearable-ble/blob/main/README.md).
- Reported results: "transcription, diarization, speaker labeling, and media tagging all ran successfully together against real captured audio … Diarization accuracy on multi-speaker recordings and the media-tagging heuristic's real-world usefulness are both still only lightly tested (so far: one short single-speaker clip)". Environment fixes needed: pin `av==16.1.0` (faster-whisper 1.2.1 incompatible with newer PyAV) and install system FFmpeg — [README](https://github.com/jkmills/bee-wearable-ble/blob/main/README.md); [pyproject.toml](https://github.com/jkmills/bee-wearable-ble/blob/main/pyproject.toml) (deps: faster-whisper ≥1.2.1, pyannote-audio ≥4.0.7, panns-inference ≥0.1.1, bleak ≥3.0.2).
- The repo is new and small: created 2026-10-16, 3 commits, 0 stars at time of check — [GitHub API for jkmills/bee-wearable-ble](https://github.com/jkmills/bee-wearable-ble).
- For comparison, our Mac client uses `mlx-community/whisper-large-v3-turbo` via mlx-whisper and skips chunks quieter than −50 dBFS overall, because "Whisper hallucinates on silence" — [our transcribe.py](../../../../mac/src/beekit/transcribe.py).

### Inferences
- None of jkmills' stack runs on iOS as-is (Python, CTranslate2, PyTorch). What carries over is the design: VAD-gated ASR, diarization that emits per-speaker embeddings, persistent enrolled voiceprints with a cosine threshold, and optional media/TV flagging. The iPhone equivalents (SpeechAnalyzer, WhisperKit/SpeakerKit, FluidAudio/pyannote CoreML, SoundAnalysis classifier) are covered by other researchers.
- The `(old+new)/2` update over-weights the latest recording, and the 0.6 threshold is untuned. Both are weak spots worth fixing in our version, for example a proper running mean or cluster centroid with per-device threshold calibration.
- pyannote 3.1 is the older pipeline. pyannote.audio 4.x (which jkmills already depends on) ships newer pipelines (for example "community-1"). This is from memory and wasn't re-verified this session.

### Gaps
- No quantitative accuracy (WER/DER/speaker-ID precision) on Bee audio was published by anyone found.
- How 8 kbps versus 32 kbps Bee audio affects ASR/diarization accuracy is untested.

## 6. BLE throughput, connection parameters, packet sizes, and behaviour when the phone app is backgrounded

### Takeaway
Bee audio needs only about 32 kbps (4 KB/s), a small fraction of BLE capacity. On macOS (MTU 498) the device sends 495-byte notifications about every 120 ms (about 8.2/s) with zero loss over 45 s. The iPhone's negotiated MTU and connection interval for the Bee haven't been measured yet. The official app's known weakness is disconnects when the app is killed. iOS keeps BLE notifications flowing to a backgrounded `bluetooth-central` app but won't relaunch one the user force-quit.

### Cited Findings
- [HW-ours] 366 notifications over 44.82 s, each exactly 495 B (= MTU 498 − 3 B ATT header). Inter-arrival times: min 28 ms, median 120 ms, mean 122.8 ms, max 210 ms. Zero lost sequence numbers, zero resync bytes — our capture (`captures/20261020-201740-raw.jsonl`); [our PROTOCOL.md](../../../../docs/PROTOCOL.md).
- [Static] The official Android app requests MTU 517 (`gatt.requestMtu(517)`) and uses write-with-response on control. Its flow is connect → MTU → discover → subscribe. GATT ops are serialised one at a time, retried after 100 ms, and the link is torn down after repeated failures — [jkmills PROTOCOL.md](https://github.com/jkmills/bee-wearable-ble/blob/main/PROTOCOL.md).
- [Static] On Android the BLE stack runs in a separate foreground `BeeBackgroundService`, "so the wearable stays connected and controllable while the RN JS layer is backgrounded or killed" — [jkmills PROTOCOL.md](https://github.com/jkmills/bee-wearable-ble/blob/main/PROTOCOL.md).
- [Reported] iOS user: "The pioneer disconnects ALL THE TIME if Bee isn't in the background/foreground apps … I frequently kill my apps" — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/bee-your-personal-ai/id6480349491).
- Apple's Accessory Design Guidelines (section 49.6) set the minimum BLE connection interval at 15 ms. Parameter requests outside the rules may be rejected, and iOS then falls back to defaults — [Silicon Labs: connection parameters for Apple devices](https://docs.silabs.com/bluetooth/8.3.0/mobile-apps-suitable-connection-parameters); [Apple Developer Forums thread](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/773555).
- Apple's Core Bluetooth background guide: with `bluetooth-central` the app is woken for notifications in the background, and state preservation/restoration relaunches the app for BLE events after the *system* terminates it, but not after the user force-quits it — [Apple Core Bluetooth Programming Guide, background processing (legacy archive doc; not re-fetched this session)](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/NetworkingInternet/Conceptual/CoreBluetooth_concepts/CoreBluetoothBackgroundProcessingForIOSApps/PerformingTasksWhileYourAppIsInTheBackground.html).
- [HW-ours] Our iOS app already pairs via AccessorySetupKit, uses `bluetooth-central` background mode with state restoration, and reconnected in the background on the real device. BeeKit reads `peripheral.maximumWriteValueLength(for: .withoutResponse)` but so far only for SMP — [BluetoothTransport.swift](../../../../app/FreeBee/BeeKit/Sources/BeeKit/BluetoothTransport.swift).
- [Reported] Omi's iOS app had a "background audio keep-alive" for BLE devices that was changed and then reverted in Aug 2026 ("Revert 'fix(ios): stop background audio keep-alive and back off BLE timeouts'") — [Omi commit 236a46bf](https://github.com/BasedHardware/omi/commit/236a46bf). Omi's desktop Bee connector waits 1 s after connect before subscribing — [Omi BeeDeviceConnection.swift](https://github.com/BasedHardware/omi/blob/main/desktop/macos/Desktop/Sources/Bluetooth/Connections/BeeDeviceConnection.swift).
- [Reported] Phone-side drain: an Omi user streaming a Bee to the Omi app (with cloud transcription) saw the phone fall from 73% to 20% between 11:30 and 14:28 — [Omi featurebase post](https://omi.featurebase.app/p/bee-and-omi-battery-issues). This was Omi with cloud upload, not local processing. The "firmware 1.0" mentioned there is Omi's placeholder: before Omi PR #5744, Omi hard-coded "1.0.0" for Bee — [Omi PR #5744](https://github.com/BasedHardware/omi/pull/5744).

### Inferences
- The packet pattern shows the firmware fills each notification to (ATT MTU − 3) before sending. On iPhone, if the negotiated MTU is lower (for example 185 → 182-byte notifications), expect about 22 notifications/s at 32 kbps. That is still trivial, but the reassembler must not assume 495-byte packets. The official iOS app works, so the firmware must handle whatever MTU iOS negotiates.
- Bandwidth isn't the risk. Link continuity is: background lifecycle, force-quit, and iOS radio coexistence (Wi-Fi/AirPods). Each connection gap loses audio permanently (Q3), so reconnect latency matters most.
- Hardware VAD, Opus or a lower bitrate wouldn't reduce iPhone radio cost much, because the connection's radio duty cycle is dominated by the connection interval, not 4 KB/s of payload. Phone battery cost will come mainly from CPU/ANE work (decode, VAD, ASR) and from the app staying awake.
- Battery-efficient design: run only VAD continuously, batch ASR on speech segments, and do heavy work (diarization, summarization) while charging. That pipeline is covered by other researchers.

### Gaps
- The iPhone 17 Pro / iOS 27 negotiated ATT MTU, connection interval and PHY for the Bee haven't been measured. Log `maximumWriteValueLength(for: .withoutResponse)` (= MTU − 3) after connect and the per-notification byte count. A PacketLogger iOS trace shows the interval and PHY.
- Whether audio drops while our app is backgrounded or the screen is locked for hours on iOS 27 is unmeasured. Run an overnight test that counts sequence gaps and reconnects.
- The connection parameters the Bee requests (peripheral latency, supervision timeout) aren't documented.

## 7. Amazon's post-acquisition changes that affect our approach

### Takeaway
Amazon (acquisition July 2025) has kept Bee running and expanded it: Frontier and Eclipse hardware (Oct 2026), Bee Private Compute on AWS Nitro (Sept 2026), Sign-in with Amazon, a $11.99/mo Bee Pro tier, and frequent iOS app releases. No Pioneer shutdown was found. The big protocol change for third parties is that firmware ≥ 0.6.1 encrypts audio (AES-128-CTR), which broke Omi's direct Bee support. Firmware updates through the official app are the main threat to our setup. Our unit's MCUboot secondary slot still holds 0.5.0, which is pre-encryption according to Omi.

### Cited Findings
- Amazon launched Bee Frontier ($74.99, dual mics, up to 7 days, USB-C, IP67) and Bee Eclipse ($99.99, magnetically attaches to the back of a phone, "available soon") on Oct 15, 2026. TechCrunch calls it the first update to the Bee lineup since Amazon acquired the startup in July 2025. Bee Pro costs $11.99/month, and the free tier keeps 14 days of memory — [TechCrunch, Oct 15 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/15/amazons-bee-launches-two-new-personal-ai-devices-starting-at-75/); [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/10/16/amazon-debuts-revamped-bee-ai-wearables-us12-a-month-subscription-plan); [Amazon Developer News, Oct 18 2026](https://community.amazondeveloper.com/t/oct-18-2026-new-bee-devices-fire-tv-apis/29777).
- "Bee Private Compute" (Sept 23, 2026) is built on the AWS Nitro System and NitroTPM, with a Trail of Bits audit and Sigstore transparency. Bee says "Only you hold the keys" and "Amazon has no master key". Sign-in with Amazon was introduced for new users, and existing Apple/Google sign-ins continue — [Bee blog](https://bee.computer/blog/private-ai). The Frontier page also lists "On-device processing" as a feature, without detail — [Frontier page](https://bee.computer/products/frontier).
- [Static] The Android app already sent an `x-amazon-access-token` header to Bee's backend and has an attestation-wrapped OkHttp client (Play-Integrity-style). The per-device audio key comes from Block Store or from `POST https://{endpoint}/ble/secure-key` — [jkmills PROTOCOL.md](https://github.com/jkmills/bee-wearable-ble/blob/main/PROTOCOL.md).
- The iOS app is still actively shipped: v1.2.161 on Oct 20 2026, developer "Bluush Inc", with frequent "minor bug fixes" releases and a "subscriptions" release (1.2.158) — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/bee-your-personal-ai/id6480349491).
- Omi added Bee Pioneer support in Nov 2025, with plaintext ADTS and a 2-byte header stripped from each notification — [Omi PR #3339](https://github.com/BasedHardware/omi/pull/3339); [Omi bee_connection.dart](https://github.com/BasedHardware/omi/blob/main/app/lib/services/devices/connectors/bee_connection.dart). In Mar 2026 Omi added a warning that "Bee firmware >= 0.6.1 … uses AES-128-CTR encrypted audio that Omi cannot decode", advising users to "downgrade your Bee firmware to a version below 0.6.1" — [Omi PR #5744](https://github.com/BasedHardware/omi/pull/5744). Omi support (Feb 2026) advised "avoiding Bee firmware updates, which may disrupt Omi support" — [Omi featurebase](https://omi.featurebase.app/p/bee-and-omi-battery-issues).
- [HW-jkmills] The newest publicly reported Pioneer firmware is 0.6.5 — [jkmills PROTOCOL.md](https://github.com/jkmills/bee-wearable-ble/blob/main/PROTOCOL.md); [our IOS_PLAN.md](../../../../docs/IOS_PLAN.md). [HW-ours] Our slot 0 = 0.6.1 (active), slot 1 = 0.5.0 (bootable) — [our PROTOCOL.md](../../../../docs/PROTOCOL.md).
- [HW-jkmills] Audio only decrypts with the real backend-issued key. A self-generated key spliced into the token is accepted by the control plane but "does NOT propagate to the device's real-time audio encryption engine" — [jkmills PROTOCOL.md](https://github.com/jkmills/bee-wearable-ble/blob/main/PROTOCOL.md). Our token survived re-pairing the Bee to the Mac — [our PROTOCOL.md](../../../../docs/PROTOCOL.md).
- Unreliable sources to ignore: Omi issue #3539 was explicitly drafted by Grok. It claims "AES-128-CCM … key derived from the LTK", "raw PCM", firmware "1.0.2", "Hey Bee" wake word, and a locked bootloader, all contradicted by hardware-confirmed AES-CTR + ADTS AAC on 0.6.x — [Omi issue #3539](https://github.com/BasedHardware/omi/issues/3539). bytee.org's "2026 review" describes a different form factor and is unreliable (flagged in search results) — [bytee.org](https://bytee.org/amazon-bee-wearable-review-2/).

### Inferences
- Our approach (captured real token in the Keychain, AES-CTR decrypt, ADTS decode on the phone) works on 0.6.1 today. The main risks are (a) a forced firmware update that changes the key/session scheme or removes the legacy path, and (b) the official app re-pairing and re-keying the device, which would invalidate our captured token. Keep the official app logged out or deleted, and keep our iPhone as the only bonded central.
- Speculative and risky: MCUboot slot 1 holds 0.5.0, which by Omi's account should stream plaintext ADTS (2-byte header) with no key or session. In theory an SMP "test" swap could revert to it. That would mean SMP write ops (outside our read-only policy), could brick or reset pairing, might be blocked by downgrade protection, and 0.5.0 may lack features or fixes. Only consider it if the key path breaks, and only after a full PacketLogger capture of the current state.
- Frontier (0xBEEF, hw rev 3/4) is the current hardware. The Pioneer is a legacy product, so future firmware for it is likely rare, but Amazon still controls OTA through the official app.
- Amazon's privacy pitch (audio processed in memory, never stored) supports the conclusion that the device has no audio archive (Q3).

### Gaps
- No official statement on Pioneer support lifetime, end of firmware updates, or legacy-app shutdown was found.
- 0.6.2–0.6.5 release notes, and whether 0.6.5 changed the default bitrate or the encryption, aren't public.
- Whether MCUboot on the Bee has downgrade prevention, or whether 0.5.0's audio format really matches Omi's 2-byte-header parser, is unverified.
